The problem

The official numbers tell only half the story

Most wildlife collisions never make it into a database. The animal runs off, the damage looks minor, or the driver simply doesn't know that reporting is required. What gets recorded is a fraction of what actually happens out on the road.

Best estimate of the real figure
20,000+
wildlife collisions on Norwegian roads each year
Roughly 55 a day, every day of the year.

How we get to that number

  • 14,400 moose, deer and roe deer were logged in Norway's national cervid registry in 2025.
  • More than 17,000 wildlife collisions were filed with insurers the same year.
  • The gap between those two figures alone shows the registry is missing cases.
  • Then come the collisions that reach neither the police nor an insurer.
  • Norway's Institute of Transport Economics says outright that many collisions go unrecorded.

The solution

A watch on the road that never blinks

VeiVakt is a compact unit that mounts on existing poles along high-risk stretches. It keeps watch through darkness, fog, snow and temperatures far below freezing, and it reacts the instant something happens.

Detects

The hazards that kill on Norwegian roads

Animals in the carriageway, stopped vehicles, crashes, debris and rockfall. The system tells them apart and responds to each according to how dangerous it is.

Warns

Every driver, right where it matters

Warning lights come on at the hazard itself. Nothing to download, nothing to subscribe to, no new car needed — the light reaches everyone who drives past.

Alerts

Emergency services, automatically

Detect a crash and the system calls it in with an exact location. That cuts response time where minutes decide the outcome.

Documents

A record of what happens on the stretch

Road authorities get a live picture of every incident: what, where and when. It shapes the immediate response and shows where longer-term investment should go.

How it works

From incident to warning in under two seconds

Every unit makes its own call on the spot. Detection and warning run without the cloud, so the system keeps working where mobile coverage doesn't.

STEP 1 — SEE

Eyes on the road, around the clock

The unit watches the stretch day and night, in any weather. It catches what a passing driver never would.

STEP 2 — UNDERSTAND

Work out what's happening

AI decides what it is looking at and how serious it is. A moose in the lane, a car in the ditch and a plastic bag get very different responses.

STEP 3 — WARN

Light it up on the spot

Warning lights come on along the road, the road authority is notified, and a crash triggers an alert to emergency services — all inside two seconds.

Units talk to each other along the stretch. When one picks up a hazard, drivers are warned well before they reach it, not at the point where braking is no longer an option.

Who it's for

Built for the people who run Norway's roads

VeiVakt is made for the stretches where fencing, signage and mirrors don't do the job — and where a single serious crash costs far more than preventing it would have.

Norwegian Public Roads Administration

Wildlife hotspots on national and European routes, rockfall-prone sections, and stretches with a history of serious crashes.

Nye Veier

Norway's state road developer, building safety into new highways with measurable effect on sections of known risk.

County councils

County roads see the most wildlife collisions on the smallest budgets. VeiVakt targets known hotspots for a fraction of what fencing costs.

Insurers and the public purse

Norway values a single prevented road death at NOK 35–45 million. At a known hotspot, prevention pays for itself fast.

Research and partnerships

Built alongside Norway's research community

VeiVakt is a partner in WILDETECT, a research project funded by the Research Council of Norway. The consortium brings together SINTEF, the Norwegian Institute for Nature Research, the Norwegian Public Roads Administration and the Western Norway University of Applied Sciences to trial roadside wildlife detection under real Norwegian conditions.
